What you get

  • A 14 to 18 second 1920x1080 MP4 on a near-black ground, moving through the claim, the headline, an instrument readout, the claim decomposed into three measured boxes, and the lockup.
  • The editable project plus a single furniture spec for hatch angle and pitch, handle sizes, dash lengths, strip pitch, rule weights, and grade settings, so later films match exactly.
  • The rule set that makes the look hold: nothing floats, the furniture is specified once and reused, and the grade is a single pass over everything rather than a treatment per scene.

Good fit

  • Your claim is quantitative — a speed, a cost reduction, a tolerance, a throughput, a coverage number.
  • You can source every figure that goes on screen. The apparatus is a promise that they were measured.
  • You expect to make more than one, and want them to look like the same drawing.

Not for

  • Warm, human, or lifestyle products. Drafting furniture around a claim about how something feels is a joke the film is not making.
  • Claims you cannot substantiate. This structure is unusually good at making an invented number look adjudicated, which is exactly why it is the wrong place to put one.
  • Anyone wanting to show a competitor's interface or mark inside the apparatus. Every surface here is yours or a neutral original.
